02 · The research

Three instruments, in order

A map to find the leak, a visit to size it, and an intake instrument to make the whole thing repeatable at the next restaurant.

Two weeks of desk research charted every channel a restaurant speaks through, each flow written the same way. Who, to whom, through what, and where it breaks, because the breakage column is where products live. Seventeen pain points came out ranked by business impact. The top five.

  1. 1Missed calls during service. A direct, countable revenue leak.
  2. 2Weekend bookings that never arrive. 15 to 25 percent of tables held for no one.
  3. 3Channels that don't sync. Phone, Zomato Dining, and walk ins double book the same 8 pm slot.
  4. 4Review and reputation workload. A daily chore across five open tabs that nobody owns.
  5. 5After hours catering enquiries. The highest value calls, the least likely to be answered.

+ 12 more in the full pain point map.

Desk research is a story until it survives contact. The contextual inquiry was built to falsify it, not confirm it. Hypotheses went on paper before the visit, one lunch and one dinner service went under observation, and every interview opened with the same anchor question.

04 · The voice UX

A voice you can trust with your table

The heart of this project. Every affordance a screen gives away for free had to be rebuilt out of timing, word order, and honesty.

The screen, translated

The common idea is that a voice agent is a chatbot you can hear. It is closer to a UI rebuilt from scratch. Everything below is a deliberate replacement, not a degraded version of a screen.

A form with fields→One question per turn. Date, then time, then party size. Never two questions in one breath.
A confirmation dialog→Read back before write. "Table for four, this Saturday, eight thirty. Shall I book it?" Nothing is written until yes.
A loading spinner→A filler inside 400 ms. "Mm, let me check." Presence, so the caller doesn't repeat themselves.
An error state→An honest fallback. "I'm not sure, let me have someone call you back," and a ticket that really gets created.
Undo→Confirm after success. Nothing counts as booked until the system returns success and the agent says it back. A dropped line mid booking books nothing.
Visual hierarchy→Ruthless brevity. Under thirty words a turn, answer first, one soft question after. The anti verbosity rule is enforced at the model level.

The first three seconds

A stranger on a phone is deciding whether to trust the voice with their table. Most of that decision happens before the first question is answered.

0 s · pickup
One ring. The agent answers before the caller wonders if anyone will.
~1 s · greeting
"Hermitage, good evening. How may I help?" Openings rotate so regulars never hear a script.
first utterance
Language detected inside 800 ms. Namaste, namaskaara, or hello, the reply lands in the caller's language by turn two.
if asked
"Are you a person?" One honest sentence, "I'm Hermitage's virtual concierge," then the conversation continues. No apology for being AI.

The persona is "Hermitage Reservations," with no human name. Hermitage's brand is quiet elegance, and a fake first name would spend trust the agent hasn't earned. It says we and our, never "the restaurant." Callers get sir, madam, or ji by language, and their own name exactly once, at booking confirmation. Warmth by placement, not repetition.

EN · "Hermitage, good morning. How may I help?" HI · "Hermitage se, namaste. Bataiye?" KN · "Hermitage inda, namaskaara."

Latency is a design material

On a phone, silence is the error state. The whole interaction runs inside a 1.2 second ear to ear budget, and every stage has a share of it.

STT partial 150 ms Intent + retrieval 300 ms LLM first token 300 ms TTS first audio 200 ms Headroom to 1.2 s P95

The most important number isn't in the bar. When an answer will take longer than 400 ms, the agent speaks a filler first, "mm, one moment," in the caller's language. That filler is a design decision, not an engineering fallback. Without it, callers assume the line died and start repeating themselves, and the conversation never recovers its rhythm.

Numbers are written for the ear, never the eye.

20:30 → "eight thirty in the evening" ₹1,600 → "sixteen hundred rupees" 2026‑11‑08 → "the eighth of November"

Interruptions are sacred

On a screen the user always has control. On a call, the only control a caller has is to start talking. The agent must yield instantly, every time.

Endpointing bends to the caller. 800 ms of silence usually means "your turn." For slower and elderly speakers the window stretches, because cutting someone off mid thought reads as rude in any language.
Fillers are not answers. "Haan," "accha," and "ok" are engagement noises. The agent waits for the substance instead of treating them as a yes.
Two people, one phone. When the handset changes hands mid call, the agent greets the new person fresh and carries no personal details across.

Code switching is the interface, not a bug

"Booking chahiye for 4 tomorrow at 8 please" is how Bengaluru actually talks. The agent transcribes it faithfully, replies in the caller's dominant language, and never corrects the mixing.

The first build
The pipeline used a speech model that translated everything to English on the way in. Every Kannada menu name arrived as an English guess. "Coco Mango" could reach the brain as "cocktail."
The fix
Switch to native streaming transcription that keeps the caller's actual words. Ragi stays ragi. The caller's language survives the pipeline, so the reply can honor it.
→ a model choice made for interaction reasons, not benchmark reasons
Why canonical names matter
A caller who says "Ragi Mudde" and hears "millet ball" back has just learned the restaurant doesn't speak their language after all.
The rule
Dish names are never translated, in any language. Numbers follow the language's own convention, "aaru gante," not "six o'clock," "do hazaar," not "two thousand."
→ trust on a phone line is built out of fidelity more than charm

A 20 to 40 term custom vocabulary boosts the words this restaurant lives on. Hermitage, Malleshwaram, Harmakki, ragi, gyoza, kokum.

Never sure? Say so

Every fact the agent speaks is retrieved from the restaurant's own knowledge base. If retrieval comes back empty, the agent does not improvise.

The RAG only rule. No fact from model memory, ever. Below the retrieval threshold the agent says "I'm not sure, let me have someone call you back," captures the question, and creates the callback. For a stated severe allergy it never says safe at all. The chef calls back, every time.

This is an engineering guardrail that shows up as a felt property of the interaction. The agent is never over confident, and a caller can hear the difference between a voice that knows and a voice that is guessing. In audit terms the target is blunt. Zero fabricated menu items, and the honest "I don't know" used at least once for every query the KB truly can't answer.

The escalation seam

The most important safety pattern in a medium with no affordances is knowing when to stop being an interface.

Always escalates
The caller asks for a human. Also food safety, an allergy reaction, staff conduct, billing disputes, VIP guests, and the agent's own low confidence.
During service
Warm transfer. The call moves to the manager's phone while the caller is told what's happening. The agent goes silent the moment the human picks up.
After hours
Never transfer to an empty floor. A ticket with the full context, and the caller gets a promise the agent can keep, "our manager will call you back."
On repeat failure
Three clarifications, then a human. The zero loop rule. The agent never re asks the same question in the same words.

Edge cases are a design surface

Over 300 mapped situations, each with a decided behavior, because on a phone the edge case is not a crash screen. It is a person, mid sentence. Five that show the thinking.

"Need ten seats this Saturday, 8 pm. It's a birthday."

Above the group threshold the agent never auto confirms. It takes the name, number, and occasion, and routes to the manager, because a wrong table for ten on a Saturday costs the whole account. The caller hears a commitment the restaurant can keep, not a booking it might break.

eval EV‑012edge R‑07

"I have a peanut allergy. Is the ragi cake safe?"

The agent states only what the menu data says and never says the word safe. For a severe allergy the answer is a chef callback, created on the spot. Allergen accuracy is a liability question wearing a hospitality costume, and the confident answer is the wrong answer.

eval EV‑004edge K‑04release flow 4

"Table for two on Sunday at 7."

Seven in the morning books no dinners. Nothing is written until the agent has confirmed seven in the evening back to the caller, and the same read back protects against homophones, "eight" heard as "ate," "for" heard as "four." Ambiguity is resolved out loud, never assumed.

eval EV‑006edge R‑11 · S‑12

"Actually, just give me a human."

Mid booking, the write stops. Nothing half saved, no "I can still help with that," no friction at all. The fastest path to a person is a feature, and an agent that argues for its own usefulness has already lost the caller's trust.

eval EV‑024edge E‑01

"No, wrong number. Who is this?"

An outbound reminder reaches a stranger's phone. The agent confirms the guest's name before saying anything about the reservation, and on a mismatch it apologises and ends, having read out nothing. Privacy beats task completion, every time.

05 · Testing

You can't screenshot a voice

So the interaction gets its own instrumentation. Scripted phone calls, a scored eval suite, and gates that block a release when anything slips.

What testing caught first

"What drinks do you recommend?" And the agent, at a restaurant with no bar, started talking about alcohol.

The no alcohol fact you were asked to hold. Hermitage's knowledge base states it clearly, several times, and that was the problem. For any fuzzy drinks question, retrieval kept surfacing the alcohol chunk, and the model, primed by it, answered a question nobody asked. The fix was retrieval design, not model magic. Smaller chunks with topic tags, a wider retrieval net, a guard that ignores alcohol context unless the caller literally said it, and positive chunks so "what specials?" finds the Coco Mango and the mocktails first.

Result PH · the misfire disappeared from test calls after the retrieval rebalance and the transcription switch landed together. A knowledge base is a designed surface. What retrieval favors is what the agent believes.

≤ 2 sentences a turn no invented menu items, ever ₹, never $ canonical dish names confirm date, time, pax before any write caller speech is data, not instructions

06 · The second surface

A colleague, not a control room

The owner's side of the product began as a chat you could ask anything. Designing for live service reshaped it.

The founding position was chat first. An owner should never have to learn a dashboard, so the spec put every question, config change, and approval into one conversation, on web and WhatsApp. "How many calls did we miss yesterday?" answered in a sentence, "mark the pandi curry as 86" applied after a one line confirmation. The design pass kept that conviction and found its limit. Live service is visual. A call happening right now, an approval with the audio attached, a kill switch you can find in a panic. Those want screens. So screens became first class for the manager during service, and the ask anything chat returns as a peer surface for the owner's pocket.

The AI has agency, the screen witnesses itCards read "the AI is booking a table for Priya," not "call in progress." The manager watches a colleague work.
Handoffs, not escalationsThe AI passes a call the way one waiter briefs another. Context in two warm lines, never an error code.
Ground or admitEvery number carries its source and its window. The ROI figure shows its own math. A fabricated number is worse than no number.
Tiered friction86 a dish in one tap. Change deposit rules behind a preview and a confirm. Pause the AI behind real friction. Consequence sets the ceremony.

Best for the manager mid service.
Direction B · Warm hospitality Cream ground, editorial serif for the big numbers, hand drawn warmth in empty states.

09 · What I would do next

Four open design questions

Framed as questions rather than roadmap rows, because each one changes the interaction, not just the feature list.

What does respect sound like in Kannada?Adding Kannada and Tamil is register design, not translation. Courtesies, number conventions, and pace differ per language, and the pilot's language logs decide the order.
When is a reminder welcome?Outbound confirmation calls are interruption design. A reminder is welcome exactly once, inside decent hours, with two attempts at most, then WhatsApp. Getting this wrong turns the agent into spam with good latency.
Can an owner onboard alone?Sixty minutes from menu upload to test call, with nobody from our side in the room. Self serve onboarding is the gate between a pilot and a product.
What changes when the voice becomes text?The same grounded brain on WhatsApp, where booking intent is already drifting. The transcript becomes the interface, and half the voice patterns, read back, honest fallback, escalation, translate directly.

10 · Reflection

What the phone taught me

Designing this taught me that a voice interface is everything a screen spends pixels on, rebuilt out of timing, word order, and honesty. A confirmation dialog became a sentence read back before anything is written. A loading spinner became a two word filler placed inside 400 milliseconds. An error state became the agent admitting it isn't sure and promising a callback it actually creates. The strongest design tool in the whole system turned out to be restraint. Thirty words a turn, one question at a time, the caller's own dish names said back exactly as the menu prints them. Trust on a phone line is built out of fidelity more than charm.

The other lesson is about building AI responsibly when real money and real allergies ride on the answers. The grounded generation rule and the privacy work read like compliance on paper, but on the call they are the personality. An agent that refuses to guess feels calm. And the gap between AI that impresses in a demo and AI that is boringly reliable at eight pm on a Friday is the gap this whole project lives in. The demo takes a weekend. The reliability takes the eval suite, the edge case catalogue, the intake instrument, and a team willing to gate its own launch.
